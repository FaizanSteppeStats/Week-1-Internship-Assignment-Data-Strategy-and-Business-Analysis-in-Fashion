I am submitting my Week 6 assignment, “Reporting, Evaluation, and Presentation of Data Insights,” as a final integrated Word report with a reproducible GitHub evidence project. The submission combines work from the first five internship tasks into one clear narrative: identifying relevant fashion-industry business questions, acquiring and preparing public data, exploring patterns, evaluating forecasts, assessing data quality, and translating findings into responsible operational recommendations.

The report uses verified and clearly labelled evidence. Week 1 supplied an example of joining Indian World Bank internet-use and urban-population indicators, highlighting that national connectivity figures cannot independently prove fashion marketing conversions. Week 2 supplied a real public apparel catalog audit, including 161 collections, three missing image URLs and one missing SEO description. Week 5 supplied a separate synthetic data-quality exercise reconciling 163 apparel transaction rows into 147 accepted sales, two returns and 14 quarantined records. The report explicitly distinguishes this simulated test from actual business transactions.

For real fashion-sector time-series analysis, the submission uses 92 monthly U.S. Census clothing-store sales observations through August 2026, sourced via FRED. An exploratory comparison finds a 1.90-times December-to-February median sales relationship for 2021–2025, which is presented as a national seasonal pattern rather than proof of local SKU demand in India. The forecasting evaluation independently recomputes accuracy from eight held-out months in January–August 2026. The selected seasonal-trend method achieved 2.66% WAPE compared with 5.69% for a seasonal-naïve baseline, while still showing an average underprediction of USD 484.38 million per month. The report explains why both forecast bias and short test windows matter for deployment.

I provide original charts, an evidence-to-action matrix, model-evaluation tables, a six-week implementation plan, decision owners, proposed KPIs, explicit stopping rules, and practical presentation guidance for executives, merchandising teams and technical reviewers. Five specific ethical and operational failure cases cover private customer identities, size availability bias, geographic transferability, overconfident predictions and data revisions. The GitHub folder includes source snapshots, Python code, historical predictions, independent metric calculations, four figures, reusable CSV/JSON outputs and nine passing tests. All proposed local business initiatives are identified as untested pilots rather than claimed improvements.
