I am submitting my Week 1 report, “Fashion Data Strategy and Business Analysis,” prepared for the Junior Data Science Analyst Internship in Coimbatore. The report presents a practical, research-based strategy for using data science to support decision-making across the apparel, textiles and fashion industry. Its main objective is to explain how a fashion business can convert fragmented data into measurable improvements in demand planning, inventory management, trend monitoring and customer marketing.

The report begins by examining the business context of the fashion sector, including changing consumer preferences, short product life cycles, the importance of size-level inventory management and the textile ecosystem of Tamil Nadu. It then establishes clearly defined strategic objectives, research questions and measurable key performance indicators. These include forecast accuracy, stock availability, sell-through, aged inventory, return rates, data completeness and incremental marketing performance. Each indicator is connected to a potential business decision, with proposed pilot thresholds identified as goals rather than achieved results.

A major section evaluates publicly accessible and permission-controlled research sources such as Google Trends, Tamil Nadu government publications, historical weather data, the H&M fashion recommendation competition and the UCI Online Retail dataset. The report explains the usefulness and limitations of each source, including source licensing, data quality, the interpretation of search trends and customer privacy.

The proposed implementation plan covers six weeks, from 13 October to 24 November 2026, with weekly work packages, deliverables, resources, acceptance checks, risks and mitigation steps. It is designed to be completed independently without requiring confidential company data.

To support the strategy with technical evidence, I am also providing a Python project that generates reproducible synthetic fashion data and demonstrates demand-forecast comparison, illustrative reorder recommendations and customer segmentation. The project includes code, sample output files, charts, a README and automated tests. Synthetic results are expressly labelled as demonstrations and are not presented as genuine company performance. Overall, the submission connects business analysis, responsible data practices, practical analytics methods and a feasible roadmap for future validation on authorised operational data.
