I am submitting my Week 4 assignment, “Predictive Analytics and Forecasting in the Fashion Industry,” with a detailed Word report and a reproducible Python implementation. This project forecasts a genuine public fashion-sector indicator: monthly U.S. clothing-store sales published by the U.S. Census Bureau via FRED. The data extend from January 2019 through August 2026, use millions of nominal U.S. dollars, and are explicitly not seasonally adjusted. The report explains why a sector-level demand indicator is useful for learning forecasting methods but cannot be interpreted as unit-level demand for an individual textile or apparel retailer in Coimbatore.

The forecasting framework documents the business problem, target definition, data acquisition and cleaning, leakage prevention, feature construction, candidate models, metric selection, training, validation, deployment and error management. I implement three methods: a 12-month seasonal-naïve baseline, an interpretable seasonal-trend method using a fixed half-year-over-year drift adjustment, and regularized Ridge regression with calendar Fourier features and January/December indicators. Model assumptions and parameter choices are described, and alternative SARIMA and tree-based techniques are discussed with their respective risks.

Validation is chronological and reproducible. The models are evaluated on two expanding-window origins: forecasting every month of 2024 using only older observations and forecasting every month of 2025 using only data available through 2024. The preferred method is selected using pooled weighted absolute percentage error. It is then evaluated once on a separate untouched January–August 2026 test set. The selected seasonal-trend method achieved 4.72% WAPE on model-selection folds and 2.66% WAPE on the separate 2026 holdout, compared with 5.69% for seasonal naïve. These are measured historical evaluation results, not claims about future profitability or local sales.

The report includes model-comparison, holdout and forward-scenario charts, plus scorecards for WAPE, MAE and forecast bias. A September 2026–August 2027 projection is supplied strictly as a forecast scenario with an illustrative uncertainty stress band, not a calibrated 95% prediction interval. Practical sections discuss COVID-like structural breaks, incomplete data releases, dataset revisions, cost-sensitive inventory decisions, overfitting and retraining triggers. The GitHub repository contains complete data, runnable scripts, output CSVs, plots, test cases and an evaluator-first navigation guide. The project requires no private company records.
